How to make a vision board for 2027 (that you will actually use)

Most vision boards end up in a drawer by February. The ones that work have three things in common: specific goals, images that show the result, and a spot where you see them every day. Here is a 15-minute method.

Short on time? Make a free digital board in 60 seconds →

1. Look back at 2026 first

Take five minutes and write down three things that went well this year and one thing you want to leave behind. This keeps your 2027 board grounded in your real life, not just Pinterest.

2. Choose 4–7 life areas

A board with 20 goals has no focus. Pick the areas that matter most right now. Popular ones: travel, money, career, health, love, self-care, home, friends, study, business, creativity, inner peace.

3. Turn each area into one specific goal

“Travel more” is a wish. “Lisbon with my sister in May” is a goal. For every area, write one line you could check off by December. Numbers help: save $5k, read 24 books, run a half marathon.

Quick test: could a friend tell, on December 31, whether you did it? If not, make it more specific.

4. Pick a word of the year

One word that sums up how you want 2027 to feel: abundance, softness, focus, adventure, peace, discipline. It goes under the title and acts as a tie-breaker when you are deciding how to spend your time.

5. Choose an aesthetic you love looking at

You will look at this board hundreds of times, so it should feel like you. Some popular 2027 looks: clean girl, coquette, old money, mob wife, dark luxury, cozy winter and minimalist. See all 18 looks.

6. Collect images that show the result

Choose photos of the outcome, not the effort: the view from the hotel balcony, not the plane ticket. One image per goal is enough. Your own photos (your city, your dog, your friends) make it personal.

7. Add 2–3 short affirmations

Present tense, short enough to read in a glance: “Money flows to me easily”, “I keep my promises to myself”, “Soft life era”. Need ideas? Here are 152 affirmations grouped by goal.

8. Put it where you will see it every day

This is the step most people skip. A wall board is great, but your phone lock screen is the place you look most often, around 100 times a day. 9. Review it every quarter

Put four dates in your calendar: end of March, June, September and December. Check off what is done, swap what no longer fits, and refresh the look so you notice it again. A printable goals checklist with a monthly check-in row makes this easy.

Physical vs digital: which is better?

Both work, and they work best together. A physical board is a fun ritual (vision board parties are great for this). A digital board goes everywhere with you. Glowboard makes both from the same design: a phone wallpaper for free, plus 4K desktop and printable A4 versions with Pro.
